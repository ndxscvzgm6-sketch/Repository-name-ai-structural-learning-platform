Lecture four. The precast design-review exercise. You're the owner's engineer. A precaster's sixty-percent submittal lands: insulated sandwich wall panels for a data hall, total-precast electrical annex, hollow-core roof. I'll read you five excerpts; after each, pause and decide what you'd flag. Then I'll give the answer. This is a rehearsal for the real thing.

Excerpt one. The sandwich panel calc states: panels designed as fully composite sections; wythe connectors: proprietary polymer pins at twenty-four inches each way; thermal analysis: not included, see architect. Pause. What's wrong? Answer: fully composite is a claim, not a default. Polymer pin systems are usually partially composite, the percentage comes from the manufacturer's testing, and claiming one hundred percent both overstates stiffness and understates the connector forces. And a fully composite panel maximizes thermal bowing, which is precisely why the thermal analysis can't be see architect. Findings: provide the tested percent-composite basis, recompute service deflections and connector demands, and own the bowing calc, sun on one face of a forty-foot composite panel is a structural load case here.

Excerpt two. The connection schedule shows every panel with four welded plates, two top, two bottom, all detailed identically: full-strength welds, no slots. Pause. Answer: that's a hard-point grid, a volume-change trap. A hundred-plus feet of wall, fully welded, will fight its own shrinkage until something cracks or tears. Findings: identify the movement plan, convert lateral connections to slotted or flexible details, designate bearing versus tie-back roles explicitly, and show the accumulated-movement calculation per run.

Excerpt three. Bearing detail: hollow-core planks bear two inches on precast ledges, no pad shown, grout solid at bearing. Pause. Answer: two problems. Two inches is at or below minimum unfactored bearing for hollow-core, check the handbook minimum plus tolerance erosion, a half-inch of tolerance can nearly halve real seating. And hard-grouted bearings with no pad restrain rotation and slip, spalling factory. Findings: verify bearing length with tolerances, add designed bearing strips, and justify any grouted-solid joints against movement.

Excerpt four. The seismic narrative: lateral resisted by precast shear walls; connections designed for calculated forces per the elastic model. Pause. Answer: the missing word is ductility. Intermediate precast walls require connections that yield at one and a half times yield or are capacity-protected; designed for calculated forces satisfies neither. Findings: classify the system, provide the connection ductility or overstrength justification, and confirm diaphragm chords and collectors across the plank roof, hollow-core diaphragms need explicit chord steel and topping or connector shear paths.

Excerpt five. The interface note: foundation embeds by others; precast erector to verify. Pause. Answer: that sentence has ended careers. By others without a named survey, tolerance, and correction protocol produces field-welded improvisation at fifty locations. Findings: require an embed placement drawing with tolerances, a pre-erection survey hold point, and an engineered correction detail for out-of-tolerance embeds, before erection, not during.

Score yourself. Five excerpts, five findings: composite assumptions, movement plans, bearing details, connection ductility, and interface protocols. Notice none of them required computing a single member: precast review is a pattern-recognition discipline, and you now hold the patterns.
