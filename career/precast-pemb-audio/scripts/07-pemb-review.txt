Lecture seven. The P E M B design-review exercise, and closing the series. Same drill as precast: five excerpts from a metal-building package for a data center campus support building, generator-adjacent warehouse, category-four campus, moderate seismic, you flag them first.

Excerpt one. The quote sheet: building designed per standard commercial criteria, collateral load three p s f, risk category two. Pause. Answer: three findings in one line. Collateral: the mechanical drawings show sprinklers, tray runs, and unit heaters totaling eleven p s f, quadruple the allowance. Risk category: campus basis of design says essential support facilities at category four, importance factors change wind, snow, and seismic. And per standard criteria means the manufacturer's defaults, not your spec. This excerpt is the whole discipline in miniature: the building will be flawlessly engineered to the wrong inputs.

Excerpt two. Foundation drawings note: designed for reactions per preliminary letter R one, dated March. The manufacturer's final letter R three, dated June, shows corner column uplift increased forty percent after the collateral revision. Pause. Answer: version control, the classic. Findings: re-verify all footings against R three, especially uplift, metal buildings are light, and wind uplift on foundations governs constantly; light buildings need heavy anchors or footing mass, and a forty percent uplift bump can flip a footing from fine to floating.

Excerpt three. Thrust resistance: hairpins into slab on grade, per standard detail. Site phasing: building erected and operational before yard slab completion; future duct bank crosses the slab at column line three. Pause. Answer: the hairpin assumption fails twice, no slab at erection, cut slab later. Findings: replace with tie rods or moment footings at affected lines, or sequence and protect, and put the thrust system on the drawings as a permanent, do-not-cut notation.

Excerpt four. Elevation shows a new twelve-foot louver opening cut into the braced bay at line one, coordination note: contractor to relocate rod bracing as required. Pause. Answer: contractors do not relocate the lateral system as required. Findings: opening moves, or the manufacturer's engineer redesigns the bracing, portal frame substitution, adjacent-bay bracing, with revised reactions cascading to, yes, the foundations again.

Excerpt five. Roof plan adds a future solar-ready zone and two rooftop units, note: units under two thousand pounds, no structural review required. Pause. Answer: weight is the smaller issue. Findings: concentrated loads on light purlins need the manufacturer's check regardless of totals; unit curbs interrupt the standing-seam bracing of purlins; and solar rails change uplift and bracing patterns across whole bays. On a P E M B, no structural review required is never a note a reviewer accepts for roof additions.

Score yourself again, five for five is the standard, and notice the meta-pattern across both exercises in this series: precast fails at movement, bearings, and interfaces; metal buildings fail at inputs, versions, and modifications. Neither fails much at member strength. That's why review literacy, not design virtuosity, is what the owner-side seats actually run on.

Close the series with your interview lines, earned honestly now. On precast: I audit sandwich panels starting from percent composite and the movement plan. On metal buildings: I treat collateral load and reaction versioning as the first two findings until proven otherwise. And the umbrella line for both, plus your anchorage series: delegated design is where data center structures live, so I've trained specifically as the engineer who writes the inputs, audits the outputs, and owns the interfaces. That sentence is the job. Congratulations on finishing the series, and go add these to the checklist page you're going to publish.
