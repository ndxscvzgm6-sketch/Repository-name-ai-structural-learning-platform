Lecture seven. Worked calculation one: the two-and-a-half megawatt generator. Follow the numbers by ear; the goal is the shape of the calc, every step, in order, with realistic magnitudes. Then reproduce it on paper as your portfolio piece.

The setup. A diesel generator set, two and a half megawatts, on grade in the yard. Operating weight, machine plus skid plus fluids: call it sixty thousand pounds, thirty tons. It sits on spring isolators, seismically rated with snubbers, on a reinforced housekeeping pad, eight inches thick, doweled to a thick yard slab. Site: design acceleration S D S one point zero, risk category four, so importance one point five. Center of gravity: fifty inches above the pad. Anchor group: the skid provides sixteen anchor locations, eight per side, on a footprint about thirty feet long by eight feet wide.

Step one, the force. On grade, z is zero, height amplifier is one. Isolated equipment: amplification two point five, response factor two point five, they cancel. So F p equals zero point four, times S D S one point oh, times importance one point five, times weight: sixty percent of the weight. Zero point six times sixty thousand: thirty-six thousand pounds lateral. Check the cap: one point six times one point five is two point four times weight, not close. Vertical component rides along: twenty percent of weight, twelve thousand pounds, up or down.

Step two, the isolator question. Snubbers with a small nominal gap: the code doubles the design force on the connection when impact is possible, our snubbers are rated with soft pads and documented gap under a quarter inch, so we design the anchorage for the doubled force at the snubber interface per the isolator manufacturer's seismic report, and the sixteen anchors for the system force with the isolated-equipment factors already included. On review, this paragraph, who took the doubling, the snubber or the anchors, is exactly where generic calcs fall apart.

Step three, distribute. Lateral thirty-six kips across the long direction: sixteen anchors, two and a quarter kips of shear each, trivial. Short direction governs overturning: force at fifty inches above the pad, moment: thirty-six kips times fifty inches, eighteen hundred kip-inches. Resisting: weight, and use the reduced vertical case, dead load times point nine minus the vertical earthquake share, about forty-two kips effective, times half the eight-foot width, forty-eight inches: about two thousand kip-inches. Margin positive, but slim: with the code's required combination the anchors see modest net tension on the windward row, call it one and a half kips per anchor, plus the two-plus kips of shear. Small numbers, but sixteen of them, in both directions, documented.

Step four, the concrete side. Three-quarter-inch cast-in headed bolts, six-inch effective embedment into the pad. Single-anchor breakout in cracked four-thousand concrete: on the order of twelve to fifteen kips, but group and edge clipping at the skid rails cuts the projected area roughly in half: call it seven kips design breakout per anchor after phi. Against one and a half tension: comfortable. Shear toward the pad edge: edge distance ten inches, breakout a few kips, against two and a quarter demand with interaction: passes, but this, predictably, is the governing ratio of the calc. Then the commandment: we claim option C, overstrength, multiply the anchor demand by omega zero of two, still passes, barely, and now the pad dowels must carry the whole thirty-six kips plus overstrength into the slab: twelve number-five dowels in shear friction, spaced along both rails.

Step five, the paper trail: anchor product and its evaluation report number, cracked and seismic qualified, special inspection required, snubber seismic certificate referenced, pad detail showing thickness, reinforcement, dowels, edge distances. That's a complete generator anchorage calc. Note what governed: not the bolt, the edge-shear breakout and the pad connection. It almost always is. Next: the chiller goes on the roof, and everything triples.
